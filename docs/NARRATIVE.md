# Folio narrative guide

_Draft, 25 October 2026. Companion to the design manifesto
([DESIGN-PLAN.md](DESIGN-PLAN.md), [the sheet](design-manifesto.png)): the manifesto says how the
site looks and behaves; this says what it says, in what order, and why anyone should keep
reading. Anything marked **(you)** is a question only the author can answer. Draft wording is
there to react to, not to keep._

---

## 1. The problem

Every page on the site is good on its own. Together they don't yet tell one story.

- **The homepage introduces a job title, not a person.** "Architectural Designer & Civil
  Engineer. Trained in both… taking concepts through to detailed 2D/3D models and drawings" is
  a CV line. Nothing on the first screen says what you care about or why the work looks the
  way it does.
- **The hero shows Semesters 1–4,** with Sem 4 as the main tile. The two newest and strongest
  semesters (5 and 6) aren't on the first screen.
- **The sections are a list of folders:** Academic, Professional, Personal, Skills, About.
  Nothing connects the engineering degree to the architecture, the 156 projects to the
  coursework, or the birds and the code to either.
- **The semesters are told unevenly.** Sem 5 and 6 are told as stories ("Searching",
  "Testing", "Yielding", "Looking back"; "Goods are counted. People are known."). Sem 1–3 are
  lists of drawing types with no "I" in them. Sem 4 is told in full, at 3,351 words.
- **About is a CV.** Job dates, two degrees, and a hobby list (drone piloting, architectural
  photography, travelling) that the rest of the site never mentions, while the things the site
  *is* full of (birds, code, manga, anime, cooking) aren't on it.

## 2. The through-line

A portfolio is easier to remember when everything on it answers one question. The work
already suggests one; three candidates, in order of preference:

### A. Counting and knowing (recommended)

> **Engineering taught me to count. Architecture is teaching me to know.**

It's already the site's own vocabulary. Sem 6's motto is "Goods are counted. People are
known." And the counting is everywhere:

| Counting (the engineer) | Knowing (the architect) |
|---|---|
| Wind load on a 14-storey tower; forty years of wind data | A courtyard you can see the sky from (Sem 6) |
| 156 projects across 19 atolls, counted by atoll | A host family who wants guests and privacy (Sem 4) |
| 15 of 204 birds drawn, live | Each bird drawn by hand, one at a time |
| A mancala bot, a Coup rating, a Titanic model | Why I kept losing, and what the bot taught me |
| A breakwater sized to the waves | An island's people who use the shore (Thulusdhoo) |

It explains the double degree without apologising for it, turns the personal work into
evidence rather than hobbies, and gives Sem 6 (the last semester) its place as the ending.

### B. Scale: from one reader to a city

Each semester is bigger than the last: one person in an 8 × 8 ft nook (Sem 1) → artists and
children in one building (Sem 2) → a ward of Malé (Sem 3) → a family and its guests on an island
(Sem 4) → a floating settlement for 450 people (Sem 5) → 84 homes on Malé's old port (Sem 6). This
is true and worth using *inside* the Academic section, but it's a description of the work, not
of you.

### C. The islands

Everything is about the Maldives: Henveiru, Kendhikulhudhoo, the lagoon, Villimalé, Thulusdhoo,
the old port, the birds. Strong as a setting; weaker as a thesis, because it says where, not why.

**(you)** Does A feel true? If it does, every section below is written to it. If not, which
sentence would you put at the top of the site instead?

## 3. Who reads it, and what they must leave with

| Reader | Time | Arrives via | Must leave knowing | Path |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Employer (engineering or architecture firm) | 1–2 min | CV link, LinkedIn | Both degrees are real; I can take a design to drawings; 156 projects of practice | Hero → Professional map → one semester → CV |
| Tutor, jury, admissions | 10–30 min | Course, application | How I think; how I've grown from Sem 1 to 6; that I reflect | Hero → Academic arc → Sem 6 → Sem 1 → Looking back |
| Peer or islander | 2–5 min | Shared link, Tumblr | That this is about places they know | Hero → a semester in their island or ward → birds |
| Someone from the code side | 3–10 min | GitHub, a coding page | That the code solves my own problems, and connects to the rest | Coding page → timeline → "This portfolio" → hero |

Two rules follow:

1. **The first screen works for the employer** (who leaves soonest): who I am, the through-line,
   the strongest image, and the two buttons that matter (see the work, download the CV).
2. **Every page works as a front door** for the other three: each says where it sits in the
   whole and where to go next (§7).

## 4. The shape of the site

The site reads as a book with a prologue, three parts and an epilogue. Folder names become
chapter titles that say something.

| Part | Now | Proposed | Its job in the story |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Hero | **Who I am, in one sentence** + the strongest image (Sem 6 or 5) | State the through-line |
| Part 1 | 01 Academic | **01 Learning to count, learning to know**: engineering (2019–23), then six semesters (2023–26) | Show the growth |
| Part 2 | 02 Professional | **02 In practice**: 156 projects, and two or three of them told | Prove it works in the real world |
| Part 3 | 03 Personal | **03 After hours**: the birds, the code, the kitchen | Show what I do unasked |
| Epilogue | Skills, About, Contact | **About me** in my own words, then skills as evidence, then contact | Make it personal, then easy to act on |

**Order inside Part 1:** engineering first, because it came first (2019–23) and because it sets
up the through-line (counting); then the six semesters as the scale arc of §2B. The homepage
rows already alternate image and text by year; keep that, and add one sentence per year saying
what changed.

## 5. Each part, in detail

### Prologue: the hero

- **Line 1, name.** Unchanged.
- **Line 2, through-line**, not a title. Draft: _"I'm a civil engineer studying architecture in
  Malé. Engineering taught me to count; architecture is teaching me to know."_
- **Line 3, the proof**, in numbers that are true: _"Six studio semesters, a 14-storey tower,
  156 projects across 19 atolls, and 15 of the Maldives' 204 birds drawn so far."_
- **Image:** the strongest current work. Today that's Sem 5 or 6, not Sem 4. **(you)** Which
  single image would you hang on a wall?
- **Buttons:** "See the work" and "Download CV". Nothing else.

### Part 1: learning to count, learning to know

**Engineering** is the prologue to the semesters, not a separate silo. Its intro should say why
an engineer started architecture. **(you)** Why did you?

**The six semesters as one arc.** Each needs a line for the arc (what it was about) and a line
for what it taught, so the section reads as growth, not a list. Drafts from the pages, with the
lesson for you to write:

| | Project | Scale | About | What it taught **(you)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Enchanting Reading Nook | one reader, 8 × 8 ft | A space to disappear into a book | |
| II | Blue Canvas | artists and children | Art and play under one roof; form found by Ricci flow | |
| III | Urban Acupuncture | a ward of Malé | Small, precise insertions instead of a master plan | |
| IV | Measured Embrace | a host family and its guests | Welcoming the world without losing yourself | |
| V | Yield, Register, Transmit | 450 people on a lagoon | A settlement that yields to the water, 2050 | |
| VI | Known Again | 84 homes, the old port | Goods are counted. People are known. | |

Every semester page then follows the five acts of the manifesto (context → the idea → how it
developed → the drawings → looking back). **Looking back is the most important chapter on the
site:** it's where the growth is stated rather than implied, and where each semester hands over
to the next. Draft hand-over lines, to be replaced by your own:

- I → II: _"The nook was for one person. Next I wanted a building for many."_
- III → IV: _"Acupuncture worked from outside in. Measured Embrace starts from the people inside."_
- V → VI: _"Yield was about water. Known Again is about the people the city forgets."_

### Part 2: in practice

The map (156 projects, 19 atolls, 33 islands) shows reach; it doesn't show what you did.

- Add **two or three projects told in a paragraph each:** the site, my part, the drawing I'm
  proudest of, what it taught me. **(you)** Which ones, and what can be shown publicly?
- Say what the engineer's job was ("I modelled, drew and coordinated…") in first person.
- Link back to Part 1 where practice and study met. **(you)** Did any job change a semester, or
  the other way round?

### Part 3: after hours

Not hobbies: the same through-line, unasked.

- **Birds.** Counting (15 / 204, live) and knowing (one bird at a time, by hand). They're also the
  site's place markers: one bird per semester. Say that out loud once, here.
- **Code.** Every entry starts from an itch: losing at mancala, a manga archive, forty years of
  wind for the thesis. The timeline already does this; its intro should say the itch is the
  point.
- **Kitchen.** Lunch Lab, from May 2027. Its "rice experiment" is also the first statistics
  project: counting again.
- **Journal (Tumblr).** The process behind Sem 5 and 6. Link it from the semester it belongs to,
  not only from the footer.

### Epilogue: about, skills, contact

- **About me**, rewritten as five or six sentences in first person, not a CV. Where you grew up
  and studied, why two degrees, what you're working towards, what you do on a free evening.
  **(you)** What are you working towards after Semester 6?
- **Hobbies** list only what the site can back up, or drop it: the birds and the code already
  say it better.
- **Skills** as evidence: each one names where on the site it's shown ("Revit: the Villimalé
  tower; Blender: Sem 5 renders; Python: the wind data").
- **Contact** ends the story with an invitation: what you're open to. **(you)** Work, study,
  collaboration?
- **Check:** About lists "2023 · Bachelor of Arts in Architectural Design", but the studio runs
  to 2026. **(you)** Is that the start year, or should it read 2023–2026?

## 6. Voice

The manifesto's P4 covers the rules (first person, sentence case, captions that describe, no
pitch). The narrative adds four:

1. **Tense.** Projects in the past ("I stacked the shelves"); practice and what's ongoing in the
   present ("I draw one bird at a time").
2. **Problem before solution.** Every page and section starts with what was wrong or wanted,
   then what I did about it. Sem 3's brief ("Malé has become a monotonous concrete jungle…")
   does this; Sem 1's standfirst doesn't.
3. **One honest admission per page.** What didn't work, a dead end, a crit comment. Sem 5's
   "Looking back" and Sem 1's "Looks too unnatural" sketches already do it; it's what makes a
   page believable.
4. **Numbers only when they're true and help.** 156 projects, 15 of 204 birds, 84 homes. Never
   round up, never "100+".

## 7. Joins between pages

The story lives in the joins as much as in the pages.

| Where | Now | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| End of each semester | Pager: "Next · Semester 2 · 2024 → Blue Canvas" | Looking back's last line hands over: one sentence on what led to the next |
| Semester → journal | Footer link only | "The studio, week by week" link inside Sem 5 and 6 (exists) and from the homepage rows |
| Semester → birds | Bird behind the title, unexplained | First mention on the homepage explains the one-bird-per-semester idea |
| Engineering → semesters | Separate cards | Engineering ends with why architecture came next |
| Coding entry → rest | "Read the full story" | Where a project fed the coursework, say so (wind data → thesis; this site → the folio book) |
| Every page → home | Navbar | Pager's middle slot: "All architecture" / "All code" (exists) |

## 8. Recurring motifs

Use these deliberately and nowhere else, so they mean something:

- **The bird:** one per semester; the collection in Part 3.
- **The progress bar:** six segments, growth across the degree.
- **Water:** the lagoon (Sem 5), the old port (Sem 6), the breakwater (engineering), the wind.
- **Malé:** Henveiru (Sem 3), Villimalé (engineering), the old port (Sem 6), 85 projects in
  Greater Malé. Home, seen three ways.
- **Counting and knowing:** the through-line, said once in the hero, once in About, and shown
  everywhere else rather than repeated.

## 9. What to cut

- The hero's job-title paragraph, once the through-line replaces it.
- Sem 1's 106-word standfirst, down to 40 (the rest moves into its story).
- Sem 4 down to its strongest 30 figures, and its two reflection sections down to one.
- The hobby list, unless it's backed by the site.
- Any sentence written to impress rather than explain ("mesmerizing aura", "leveraging").

## 10. How to write it

The writing is the slow part, and it should be. A workable order:

1. **Answer the (you) questions in this file,** in rough notes. Start with §2 (the through-line)
   and the six "What it taught" lines in §5: everything else depends on them.
2. **Write the hero and About.** Short, and they set the voice.
3. **Write Looking back for Sem 1, then 2, 3, 4.** Shortest and most personal first.
4. **Rewrite the Sem 1–3 stories** in the five acts, using the Looking back as the destination.
5. **Professional projects,** two or three.
6. **Section intros and the joins** (§7), last, once the parts exist.

Each step is a page PR checked against the manifesto's checklist; the text passes (sentence
case, alt text, typos) can run alongside without waiting.
